How This Estimated Quarterly Tax Calculator Works for Self-Employed Filers

When you get a W-2 paycheck, your employer splits payroll taxes with you and sends federal withholding to the IRS every payday. As a 1099 contractor, you handle both sides yourself.

01

The 15.3% Self-Employment Tax

W-2 employees pay 7.65% for Social Security and Medicare, and their employer matches the other 7.65%. When you work for yourself, Schedule SE requires you to pay both halves: 12.4% for Social Security (capped at $184,500 of combined W-2 and net SE earnings in 2026) plus 2.9% for Medicare with no income cap.

High earners also owe a 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax on combined earnings above $200,000 (Single / Head of Household) or $250,000 (Married Filing Jointly).

02

Why You Multiply Net Profit by 92.35%

Employers get to deduct their 7.65% payroll tax share as a business expense. To keep things fair for solo earners, the IRS lets you subtract 7.65% from your Schedule C net profit before applying the 15.3% rate—which is why Schedule SE multiplies your net profit by 0.9235.

On top of that, you deduct half of your regular self-employment tax directly from your Adjusted Gross Income before calculating federal income tax brackets.

03

How to Pay with IRS Direct Pay

You do not need paper Form 1040-ES vouchers to pay on time. Go to irs.gov/payments, choose IRS Direct Pay (free from a checking or savings account), select Estimated Tax as the reason, choose 1040-ES, and pick the 2026 tax year.

Save the confirmation number for your tax preparer or tax software next spring—you will enter the exact total you paid so it gets credited on your Form 1040.

Frequently Asked Questions About 1099 Estimated Taxes

The IRS generally requires quarterly estimated payments if you expect to owe $1,000 or more in federal tax for the year after subtracting any W-2 withholding and refundable credits. To stay within the IRS safe harbor and avoid underpayment penalties, pay at least 90% of your current-year tax bill or 100% of last year's total tax (110% if your prior-year AGI exceeded $150,000).

Missing a Form 1040-ES deadline does not trigger a flat fine, but the IRS charges an underpayment penalty calculated like daily interest on the unpaid amount from the due date until you pay. Paying as soon as you realize you missed a date stops the penalty from growing further.

Valid Schedule C business expenses—such as business mileage, software subscriptions, supplies, health insurance, and a dedicated home office—reduce your net self-employment profit before taxes are calculated. Every dollar of legitimate business deductions lowers both your 15.3% self-employment tax and your federal income tax.
